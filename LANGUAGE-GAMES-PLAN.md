# Chinese and Marathi game plan

Status: the first release is implemented as **two dedicated games**, Pip’s Chinese Adventure (`language.html?lang=chinese`) and Pip’s Marathi Adventure (`language.html?lang=marathi`). Each contains Listen & find, Word detective, My speaking turn and Sentence puzzles, plus a searchable word/phrase book and its own passport. Each offers 84 words, 40 phrases, 10 worlds and bundled audio. The additional story ideas below remain future work.

The activity-book expansion is complete and published: 36 original pictures, 108 dot-to-dot challenges and 144 picture/size puzzle combinations. The language games connect transport, picture matching and sentence puzzles to everyday language. Word-book entries with a corresponding activity template link to colouring; a full talking gallery of a child’s own drawings and the story collections remain planned.

## What we want Dhruva to be able to do

- **Marathi:** understand familiar speech, answer in short Marathi phrases, ask for things, describe his pictures, and gradually read familiar words and short sentences in Devanagari.
- **Chinese:** practise Mandarin listening and conversation alongside recognising simplified Chinese words, with optional Hanyu Pinyin support. Match vocabulary and difficulty to his actual school work once his year and current topics are known.
- Give Marathi and Chinese separate progress and difficulty choices. Strength in one language should not raise the other language’s difficulty.
- Celebrate attempts, discoveries and useful communication. The child-facing experience should be welcoming and should never label him as weak at a language.

Confirmed starting point: Dhruva is in **Kindergarten K1**; his parent describes his ability to grasp ideas as comparable to P1. Begin with K1-friendly, picture-led listening and speaking, with optional P1-style challenges. His current Chinese and Marathi comprehension and script-reading levels are still to be confirmed; strong reasoning should not automatically place him in a harder reading tier. Do not assume he can already read either script.

**Built-in audio is preferred initially.** Family recordings are not part of the first release. Offer original game audio that works without anyone at home needing to record phrases; fluent-speaker review remains necessary for learning material.

## Marathi collection

| Game | What happens | Main purpose | Proposed content bank |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pip’s Marathi Missions** | Help Pip pack a bag, find objects, serve a snack, or board the right train. Hear a short instruction, act on it, then hear and try a useful reply. | Listening that leads into speaking | 10 everyday theme packs; 80–100 useful words; 40 short model phrases |
| **My Talking Picture Book** | Reuse activity-book art. Tap an object to hear its Marathi name, hear a sentence about the picture, then describe or tell a story about it. Optional record-and-replay lets him hear his own version. | Naming, describing and speaking with a purpose | At least 24 reviewed picture prompts initially; 3 prompts per picture; draw from the existing 36-template library where suitable |
| **Akshar Adventure** | Hear a familiar word, find its picture, discover its written form, and solve a small letter/vowel-sign puzzle. Progress from small sound families to words he already knows aloud. | Connecting speech to Devanagari reading | At least 60 reviewed word cards across progressive packs; reusable listen, match, build and read-aloud activities |
| **Little Marathi Story Club** | Arrange picture panels, hear an original short story, choose what happens next, and retell it. Gradually reveal short written sentences with word-level audio help. | Connected speech and reading with understanding | 12 original stories initially, each with 3–4 panels and several retelling prompts |

Useful starting situations: family, greetings, food and drink, toys, clothes, body and feelings, animals, colours, home routines, and transport/outings. Prefer natural spoken Marathi checked by a fluent speaker. For example, a drink mission can model **मला पाणी पाहिजे.** (“I want water.”), followed later by a real request away from the screen.

Speaking progression:

1. Hear and point or act, with no reading required.
2. Hear a word and try saying it.
3. Complete a familiar short phrase with picture support.
4. Answer a question or make a request in his own words.
5. Use the same phrase with a family member during an everyday activity.

Reading progression:

1. Recognise a few familiar written words paired with pictures and speech.
2. Notice selected sounds and their written forms.
3. Explore vowel signs in small reviewed word families.
4. Read short, meaningful phrases and sentences, with help available.

Keep Devanagari letter–vowel combinations intact. Word-building puzzles need language-reviewed units rather than arbitrary splits between displayed characters. Introduce conjuncts when the learner is ready. Roman transliteration may be an optional grown-up aid; Devanagari and audio should remain visible learning targets.

## Chinese collection

| Game | What happens | Main purpose | Proposed content bank |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pip’s Mandarin MRT Missions** | Listen for colours, numbers, objects and short travel instructions. Help passengers, find a seat, pack a bag or choose a destination, then try a short response. | Familiar vocabulary, listening and conversation | 10 everyday packs; 80–100 words; 30–40 short model phrases |
| **Tone Coaster** | Hear reviewed Mandarin word recordings, match words to pictures and explore how a word sounds. Add Pinyin and tone-pattern activities when suitable for his school level. | Careful listening and sound–word connections | At least 40 reviewed listening contrasts/sets, introduced gradually; do not rely only on isolated tone drills |
| **Character Detectives** | Find a written word to match a picture, spot a known character in a scene, and later notice useful components. | Recognising simplified Chinese words and characters | At least 60 reviewed word cards; character expectations set after school level is confirmed |
| **Little Singapore Stories** | Play through school, playground, home, shop and MRT scenes. Hear a dialogue, choose a response, say a line and retell the scene. | Useful Mandarin, simple reading and conversation | 12 original mini-stories/dialogues initially, with several response and retelling prompts each |

Use familiar Singapore situations and school vocabulary where provided. Offer Pinyin and English as adjustable help. Keep tone marks accurate, review whole-phrase pronunciation, and avoid treating Pinyin alone as evidence of character reading. Any later tracing activity must use reviewed stroke sequences.

## A shared, playful learning loop

- Short optional sessions, initially roughly 3–5 minutes for K1, with 4–6 targets rather than the whole content bank at once. Offer longer sessions and P1-style challenges when he wants them.
- Begin with a tiny story goal: help Pip, make a snack, decorate a picture, deliver a parcel or finish a picture book.
- Use listening, acting, speaking and reading together, with the balance adjusted for each language and skill tier.
- Revisit familiar words in new situations. Track recognition, supported speech, independent use and reading separately; a correct picture tap should not automatically mean a word is mastered.
- Let Dhruva replay, hear again, choose easier material and revisit favourite scenes. Keep rewards about communication and creativity, with no timer or lost lives.
- End with one optional family mission, such as asking for water or explaining a drawing in Marathi.
- Content-bank counts describe distinct reviewed material. Reusing an item in several activity modes should not inflate the number of unique words or stories advertised.

## Audio and speaking

- Listening games depend on reliable audio. Prefer bundled recordings or reviewed generated audio checked by fluent speakers for both languages. Family recordings can be an optional Marathi voice pack, not a requirement to use the games.
- Browser voices can be an additional option after checking what the device supports. Never silently substitute an English or Hindi voice for Marathi, or count missing audio as a correct listening attempt.
- Start speaking practice with listen, try, replay and optional grown-up feedback. Automatic pronunciation grading is outside the initial scope; the first version should reward participation without claiming that unverified pronunciation is correct.
- Optional microphone recording should start only after an explicit tap and browser permission. Keep recordings on the device, provide playback and deletion, and allow every game to work without a microphone. Family audio must not be committed to the public repository without separate authorization.
- Keep this collection compatible with the existing static GitHub Pages website. No account or server is needed for bundled audio, local progress or optional local recordings.

## Release status and next steps

1. **K1 foundation implemented.** Unfamiliar words get a picture/audio introduction before matching. Optional reading modes and hideable sentence models offer more challenge. The first release uses original content and generated Mandarin/Marathi audio; no independent human educator review is claimed. Adapt future packs to Dhruva’s demonstrated language and script knowledge and invite fluent-speaker feedback.
2. **First playable release implemented inside two games:** 84 words and 40 phrases per language, all with included audio. Speaking acknowledgement records an attempt, not verified pronunciation. Sentence puzzles use authored Chinese word groups and whole Marathi words. Automated checks cover all content/audio pairings, rounds, progress separation and audio/recording lifecycle.
3. **Connect to the activity book:** My Talking Picture Book, beginning with Marathi. Add picture-led prompts and optional local record/replay, then a Chinese mode after its content is reviewed.
4. **Build the reading paths:** Akshar Adventure and Character Detectives, with the learner’s script knowledge guiding the starting tier.
5. **Extend conversation and stories:** Marathi Story Club, Little Singapore Stories, and Tone Coaster where appropriate. Expand each content bank with reviewed theme packs rather than releasing many nearly empty games.

Before release, check script rendering and audio on a phone, speaker replay controls, missing-audio handling, quiet/no-microphone play, separate language progress, gentle retries, saved-art reuse, and the actual amount of reviewed content. Test language-specific word boundaries and answer choices as well as the shared game rules.

This supports the proposed balance of skills; it is not a claim of alignment to Dhruva’s particular textbook or school.

The PDF could not be read directly during this planning pass, so no detailed syllabus coverage is claimed.

These are game-design proposals, not a complete Marathi course or a complete Singapore Chinese curriculum.
